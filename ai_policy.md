We do not support the use of AI to:
- serve corporate greed
- create content mills and other deceptive or harmful content
- imitate living artists' personal styles without their consent

It is not neutral to replace humans with AI in a world where most people must work to have their basic needs met and many of our energy sources do ecological and social harm. We also want to acknowledge that many, many artists' work was used without their consent to train these models.

Despite these serious issues, we generally regard generative AI with cautious optimism. 

This is partly because we are working to build a world in which people's basic needs are met regardless of their ability or willingness to work. In that world and - we believe - in this current one, generative AI like Stable Diffusion can be a tool for accessibility; something that can give many people a greater ability to express their thoughts, ideas, and visions for the future.
***
On a personal level, many members of our system used to draw and work professionally as artists. 

Our work was supported by funding for the sciences which has largely been cut off, and the work we did no longer requires a person to do it. Our job was one that was removed and replaced, even before the current generation of AI models.

In our personal life, the disability, chronic pain and fatigue we& have now also means we can't draw the way we used to. We continue to make art; the drive to create didn't vanish - just our ability to make a profit from it, along with our ability to make art by the same methods we used to, or as quickly and easily.

When we talk about generative AI as an accessibility tool, it is partly because that is how we use it.
***
Together with the sheer number of members of our system, sometimes it is more accessible for us to use AI to make sure that everyone can have a "face" to present to folks outside the system - to help you get to know us. It's still not easy or quick - using generative AI to express something genuine is a skill - but sometimes it's the best method we have access to for what we need.

Still, we want to be thoughtful about when, why and how we're using image generators, and we will continue to commission human artists whenever we can afford to. We use generative AI for members of the system for whom a Picrew has not been found that will meet their individual needs, and we will keep trying to find Picrews and commission art for members of the system who have only AI portraits out of consideration for those that are made viscerally uncomfortable by the presence of generative AI images in their spaces, and for use in spaces that choose not to allow it.
***
Something we've noticed in our use of AI is that using it makes it more difficult to express something that hasn't been depicted often in the training data. In particular, we notice that it is difficult to generate faces that are non-binary, visibly queer, middle aged, and not super attractive. Our faces are not the ones it "wants" to make - despite being white and thin; things that are notably privileged when it comes to how many images of people like us there are online.

We are moving towards a future which includes generative AI, whether we want it to or not. It is important to consider the ways in which AI shapes what we try to express with it, and how we can change and adapt it to be more flexible and push us in ways we want to go, not towards a privileged historic "norm".
